# About My Work

## From manufacturing systems to AI-assisted delivery

My career was built in operating environments where incomplete work becomes visible quickly.

I started in defense manufacturing and material support, working across receiving, expediting, inventory, quality, and manufacturing-engineering support. I later moved into high-volume consumer manufacturing, first as a technician and then as a frontline leader responsible for safety, quality, delivery, reliability, staffing, training, and day-to-day execution across multiple production lines.

Today, my manufacturing work centers on Autonomous Maintenance and equipment ownership: developing frontline capability, identifying and aging defects, improving standards, coordinating across operations, maintenance, and engineering, and turning operating data into practical action.

That background shapes how I approach software and AI.

I do not begin with "what can the model generate?" I begin with questions that are familiar from operations:

- What outcome are we actually trying to produce?
- Who or what owns each decision?
- What dependencies must exist before the work can succeed?
- What constitutes a defect versus an incomplete state?
- What evidence is sufficient to call the work complete?
- What happens when execution is interrupted, partially successful, or wrong?
- How do we prevent a correction in one area from breaking accepted behavior somewhere else?

## What I personally own in AI-assisted work

The implementation is heavily AI-assisted. I do not represent generated code as if I manually wrote it line by line.

My work is concentrated in the parts that determine whether the resulting system is useful and trustworthy:

- problem framing and product intent;
- requirements and acceptance criteria;
- technical architecture, ownership, and authority boundaries within authorized scope;
- task decomposition and sequencing;
- prompt and implementation direction;
- test and evaluation design;
- failure investigation and root-cause reasoning;
- reviewing implementation and proof outputs;
- deciding what evidence can be reused and what must be rerun;
- separating implemented, proven, deployed, and released states;
- maintaining claim boundaries when evidence is incomplete.

That is the same operating discipline I use in manufacturing, applied to a different kind of system.
